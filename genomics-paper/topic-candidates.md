# Candidate Topics for a Nature-tier Statistical Genomics Paper

| # | Working Title | Core Idea | Why It Matters | Novelty & Risk | Notes |
|---|---------------|-----------|----------------|----------------|-------|
| 1 | **Causal Diffusion Models for Single-cell Perturbation Responses** | Train a diffusion-based generative model that maps genomic context + perturbation design (CRISPRi/a, drug) + microenvironment cues to full multi-omic readouts (RNA, chromatin, proteins) across cell types. | Enables in silico prediction of gene programs under unseen interventions, accelerating drug discovery. | **High novelty**: no existing method combines diffusion generative modeling with explicit causal constraints + multi-omic conditioning. Risk: data scarcity for certain perturbations. | Combines Perturb-seq, Multiome, CITE-seq; leverages intervention labels for identifiable causal mechanisms. |
| 2 | **Foundation Priors for Population-scale Rare Variant Risk** | Use regulatory foundation models (e.g., Enformer++) to produce structured priors for Bayesian fine-mapping in diverse ancestries, improving rare variant effect estimates. | Direct impact on clinical genetics and equitable risk prediction. | **Medium novelty**: variants of this idea exist, but not yet with full-stack integration (sequence → regulatory effect → trait) and calibration per ancestry. | Requires massive compute and careful statistical calibration; more incremental than Topic 1. |
| 3 | **Spatially-resolved Counterfactual Inference** | Learn a graph neural latent process that predicts how spatial transcriptomic neighborhoods would respond to targeted perturbations (e.g., cytokine blockade) by combining spatial omics + matched perturbation assays. | Bridges spatial biology with therapeutic design; opens door to virtual tissue perturbations. | **High novelty but high data risk**: very limited paired spatial + perturbation datasets. | Would require generating/assembling new datasets; may be hard to convince reviewers without strong experimental validation. |

**Recommendation:** Topic 1 (Causal Diffusion Models for Single-cell Perturbation Responses) offers the best mix of revolutionary impact and plausibility for a Nature-level contribution. It unifies several frontier datasets, advances the statistical modeling toolkit, and has clear translational applications (predicting combinatorial gene perturbations, drug synergies, rescue strategies).
